GTM · Content Factory · Marketing Engineer

I build go-to-market systems for technical products — and the content engine that keeps them fed.

Most teams treat GTM as a launch checklist and content as something you post when there's time. I treat both as infrastructure: one system, one input a week, compounding output on every channel.

What I do

  • GTM — positioning, ICP, packaging, launch sequencing, and the sales motion that follows the launch.
  • Content factory — one long-form input a week turning into posts, clips, visuals, emails and landing pages, shipped on a schedule instead of on inspiration.
  • Marketing engineering — the plumbing that makes it repeatable: tracking, attribution, automations, agents and dashboards. I write the scripts, not just the strategy deck.

How the factory runs

1 input a week
→ 1 anchor piece (long-form, video or teardown)
→ ~40–60 assets across X, LinkedIn, email and the site
→ every asset tagged, tracked and scored
→ whatever wins becomes next week's input

No "post when inspired". No dashboards nobody reads. A system that runs whether or not I'm in the room.

Principles

  • Distribution beats features. Anyone can clone a product; almost nobody clones a channel.
  • Ship the system, not the deck. A framework that only lives in a document is a hobby.
  • Quality at volume is a process problem, not a talent problem. Templates beat genius.
  • Measure the asset, kill what doesn't compound.

Currently

Open to GTM and content-system engagements with teams shipping technical products. If your distribution is behind your product, that's the gap I close.
